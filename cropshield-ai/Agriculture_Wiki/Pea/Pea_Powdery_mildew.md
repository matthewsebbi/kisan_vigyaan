### Primary Affected Plant Parts

- leaf_blade
- stem

- whole_plant

### Symptom Class

- surface_growth

- Width profile: `variable`
- Ends: `irregular`

### Size

- white fungal growth may begin as small patches and progressively cover large portions of leaves, stems, and pods

### Color

- Center / primary color: white
- Margin: diffuse
- Surrounding tissue: green
- Color transition: `gradual`

### Texture

- Current texture: powdery, superficial

### Pattern and Distribution

- Orientation: `irregular`
- Distribution: `scattered_to_extensive`
- Coalescence: `extensive`
- Vein relationship: `not_distinctive`

### Spatial Location

- Primary location: `leaf_blade`
- Surface association: `upper_and_lower_leaf_surfaces`

### Whole-Plant / Field-Level Features

- reduced_vigor
- premature_leaf_yellowing

## Hallmark Features

- white_powdery_surface_growth
- growth_can_cover_large_leaf_areas
- affects_leaves_stems_and_pods

## Negative / Exclusion Features

These features should reduce confidence in this diagnosis when they are clearly present.

- downy_growth_restricted_mainly_to_lower_leaf_surface
- orange_to_rust_colored_pustules
- discrete_dark_lesions_with_pycnidia

## Differential Diagnosis

### Similar Problems

- Pea Downy Mildew
- Pea Rust
- Ascochyta Blight

### Diagnostic Discriminators

- Compare the affected plant part before interpreting lesion color.
- Identify whether fungal growth is powdery and superficial or downy and associated with the lower leaf surface.
- Distinguish white fungal colonies from yellow or orange rust pustules.
- Assess whether symptoms are primarily surface growth or discrete necrotic lesions.
- Treat uncertain surface orientation as uncertainty rather than a fixed diagnostic fact.

## Environmental Conditions

### Temperature

- Warm to moderate conditions can favor disease development.

### Humidity

- Moderate to high humidity can favor infection and fungal growth.

### Rainfall / Moisture

- Disease can develop under relatively dry leaf-surface conditions compared with downy mildew; humid conditions can still favor development.
