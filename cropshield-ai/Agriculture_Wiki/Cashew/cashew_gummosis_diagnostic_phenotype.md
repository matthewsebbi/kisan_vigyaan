# Cashew Gummosis

## Diagnostic Phenotype

### Primary Affected Plant Parts

- trunk
- main_branches
- scaffold_limbs
- lower_bark
- vascular_cambium

### Symptom Class

- exudation_gummosis
- bark_canker_cracking
- vascular_dieback

### Lesion / Symptom Geometry

- Primary shape: `irregular_canker_and_bark_fissure`
- Elongation: `moderate_vertical_along_trunk`
- Width profile: `variable_expanding`
- Ends: `diffuse_and_cracked`
- Discrete or continuous: `discrete_cankers_coalescing_vertically`

### Size

- Cankers range from 2–5 cm in early stages to large longitudinal fissures exceeding 30–50 cm along trunk/branches.

### Color

- Center / primary color: amber_brown_to_dark_reddish_brown_exudate
- Margin: dark_brown_to_blackened_necrotic_bark
- Surrounding tissue: greyish_weathered_bark_or_yellowing_foliage
- Color transition: `abrupt_at_bark_fissure_margin`
- Current/overall colors: translucent_amber, dark_reddish_brown, translucent_yellow, charcoal_black

### Texture

- Current texture: sticky_viscous_exudate_when_fresh, hard_glassy_brittle_when_dry, rough_corky_cracked_bark
- Early appearance: `wet_water_soaked_bark_weeping_translucent_gum`

### Pattern and Distribution

- Orientation: `vertical_longitudinal_cracks`
- Distribution: `localized_cankers_extending_along_woody_tissues`
- Coalescence: `moderate_to_extensive_along_scaffold_limbs`
- Vein relationship: `not_applicable`

### Spatial Location

- Primary location: `trunk_base_graft_unions_and_scaffold_branch_crotches`

### Whole-Plant / Field-Level Features

- bark_exudation_of_amber_gum
- canopy_thinning_and_dieback
- premature_leaf_drop
- yellowing_of_canopy_leaves
- collar_rot_and_tree_decline

## Hallmark Features

- profuse_exudation_of_amber_to_reddish_brown_gum_from_trunk_and_branches
- longitudinal_bark_fissuring_and_canker_formation
- sticky_translucent_gum_droplets_hardening_into_amber_nodules
- canopy_dieback_associated_with_vascular_occlusion

## Negative / Exclusion Features

These features should reduce confidence in this diagnosis when they are clearly present:

- angular_water_soaked_leaf_spots_without_woody_exudation
- powdery_white_mycelial_growth_on_leaf_surfaces
- rapid_blighting_of_tender_flushes_without_stem_gumming
- distinct_target_board_concentric_leaf_lesions

## Differential Diagnosis

### Similar Problems

- Anthracnose stem gummosis (colletotrichum-induced secondary exudation)
- Phytophthora collar rot
- Bark borer / insect mechanical damage exudation

### Diagnostic Discriminators

- Gummosis (*Lasiodiplodia theobromae* / *Phomopsis* spp.) shows deep longitudinal bark splitting with heavy amber gum resin, whereas secondary anthracnose gumming is usually restricted to tender twigs.
- Insect damage exhibits visible entry/exit holes and frass mixed with gum, unlike pure physiological/pathogen-driven gummosis fissures.
- Phytophthora collar rot primarily affects root crowns and soil lines with wet wood discoloration, rather than aerial canopy limb fissures.

## Environmental Conditions

### Temperature

- Optimal pathogen activity: 28°C – 35°C (high heat favors *Lasiodiplodia* development)

### Humidity

- Relative humidity > 80% accelerates canopy infection and symptom progression.

### Rainfall / Moisture

- Frequent rainfall, high humidity, and prolonged mechanical/pruning wetness favor spore germination and stem entry.

### Soil

- Poorly drained, waterlogged, or heavily compacted soils predispose trees to stress-induced gummosis.

## Crop Stage

- Vegetative phase, mature bearing trees, flush/fruiting stages (all stages with woody bark tissue).
