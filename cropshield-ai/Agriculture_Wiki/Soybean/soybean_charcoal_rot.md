# Soybean Charcoal Rot

## Diagnostic Phenotype

### Primary Affected Plant Parts

- root_system
- basal_stem
- taproot
- vascular_system

### Symptom Class

- root_decay_and_necrosis
- vascular_wilt
- microsclerotial_blackening

### Lesion / Symptom Geometry

- Primary shape: `streaked_to_girdling_decay`
- Elongation: `high_along_root_and_lower_stem_axis`
- Width profile: `expanding_to_cover_stem_base`
- Ends: `diffuse`
- Discrete or continuous: `continuous_decay`

### Size

- Discoloration and sclerotia development extend 5 cm to 20 cm above the soil line on lower stems and throughout the taproot system.

### Color

- Center / primary color: grayish_black, charcoal_gray, silvery_black
- Margin: dark_brown_to_black
- Surrounding tissue: chlorotic_yellowing_foliage
- Color transition: `gradual`
- Current/overall colors: charcoal_gray_stem_pith, silvery_black_epidermis, reddish_brown_internal_vascular_streaks

### Texture

- Current texture: dry, spongy, shredded_pith, dusty_peppery
- Early appearance: `light_brown_discoloration_of_taproot_vascular_system`

### Pattern and Distribution

- Orientation: `longitudinal_along_stem_and_root_axis`
- Distribution: `generalized_lower_stem_and_root_decay`
- Coalescence: `extensive`
- Vein relationship: `not_applicable`

### Spatial Location

- Primary location: `lower_stem_base_and_sub_surface_root_system`

### Whole-Plant / Field-Level Features

- premature_death_with_leaves_remaining_attached
- peppery_black_dusting_microsclerotia_inside_pith_and_under_bark
- top_dieback_during_hot_dry_weather
- patchiness_in_drier_sandy_or_compacted_field_areas
- reduced_seed_size_and_pod_filling

## Hallmark Features

- silver_gray_to_charcoal_black_discoloration_of_lower_stem_and_taproot
- dense_accumulation_of_tiny_black_microsclerotia_giving_a_peppery_appearance
- pith_of_lower_stem_becomes_shredded_and_hollowed_out
- sudden_plant_death_during_pod_fill_with_dead_leaves_clinging_to_petioles

## Negative / Exclusion Features

These features should reduce confidence in this diagnosis when they are clearly present.

- large_rat_dropping_like_rigid_black_sclerotia (>2 mm) inside stem pith (indicates Sclerotinia Stem Rot)
- brick_red_taproot_lesions_without_microsclerotia (indicates Sudden Death Syndrome or Fusarium)
- raised_powdery_foliar_pustules
- systemic_downy_white_growth_on_lower_leaf_surface

## Differential Diagnosis

### Similar Problems

- Sudden Death Syndrome (SDS) (*Fusarium virguliforme*)
- Brown Stem Rot (*Cadophora gregata*)
- Stem Canker (*Diaporthe phaseolorum*)
- Phytophthora Root Rot (*Phytophthora sojae*)

### Diagnostic Discriminators

- Charcoal Rot produces tiny, peppery black microsclerotia inside shredded pith and under the lower stem epidermis, whereas SDS and Brown Stem Rot do not produce microsclerotia.
- Sclerotinia Stem Rot produces fluffy white mycelium and large sclerotia, while Charcoal Rot occurs under hot, dry conditions and produces microscopic black sclerotia without white fluffy growth.

## Environmental Conditions

### Temperature

- High soil and air temperatures (30°C to 38°C) accelerate disease expression.

### Humidity

- Low atmospheric humidity; drought conditions strongly favor symptom development.

### Rainfall / Moisture

- Severe drought stress during crop flowering and pod fill triggers rapid plant collapse.

### Soil

- Compacted, sandy, low-water-holding-capacity soils intensify drought stress and disease severity.

## Crop Stage

- Pathogen infects roots early in the season, but severe symptoms and plant death occur primarily during post-flowering to pod-fill stages (R-5 to R-7).
