### Size

- Systemic; affects leaves, main pseudostem, floral stalk, and individual fruit fingers

- Primary location: `youngest_inner_leaves_or_male_flower_bud_first`

- rapid_yellowing_and_wilting_of_young_leaves

## Hallmark Features

- rapid_wilting_starting_on_youngest_leaves
- bacterial_ooze_droplets_exuding_from_cut_vascular_strands
- dry_dark_brown_internal_rot_of_fruit_pulp
- shriveled_and_blackened_male_bud_and_rachis
- petiole_breakage_with_leaf_hanging_down

## Negative / Exclusion Features

These features should reduce confidence in this diagnosis when they are clearly present.

- absence_of_vascular_ooze_when_cut_and_suspended_in_water
- progression_strictly_limited_to_older_lower_leaves
- healthy_unaffected_fruit_pulp_in_severely_wilted_mats
- circular_discrete_leaf_spots_with_grey_centers

## Differential Diagnosis

### Similar Problems

- Fusarium Wilt / Panama Disease (*Fusarium oxysporum* f. sp. *cubense*)
- Abiotic storm/wind damage
- Xanthomonas Wilt of Banana (*Xanthomonas vasicola* pv. *musacearum*)

### Diagnostic Discriminators

- Bacterial Ooze Test: Suspension of a cut pseudostem section in clean water yields visible bacterial streaming/ooze in Moko/Bacterial wilt, absent in Fusarium.
- Leaf Age Sequence: Moko often attacks young, inner leaves first, whereas Fusarium affects the oldest leaves first.
- Fruit Pulp Examination: Moko causes distinct internal dark brown decay of the fruit pulp; Fusarium leaves fruit pulp uninvaded.

## Environmental Conditions

### Temperature

- 25°C to 35°C (Warm, tropical temperatures accelerate bacterial multiplication)

### Humidity

- High atmospheric humidity promotes insect vector activity and mechanical transmission

### Rainfall / Moisture

- Wet soil conditions and rain splashing facilitate bacterial survival and transmission via roots/pruning tools

### Soil

- Transmitted through contaminated soil, irrigation water, infected suckers, and insect vectors visiting male flowers

## Crop Stage

- Susceptible at all stages, particularly critical during flowering and early fruiting due to insect transmission via male buds.
