- leaf_margin_yellowing_progressing_inward
- leaf_collapse_at_petiole_forming_skirt_around_pseudostem
- internal_reddish_brown_vascular_streaks_in_pseudostem_and_corm

## Negative / Exclusion Features

These features should reduce confidence in this diagnosis when they are clearly present.

- bacterial_ooze_from_cut_vascular_strands
- bacterial_wilt_associated_fruit_rot_or_internal_pulp_browning
- rapid_wilting_of_youngest_sucker_leaves_first
- presence_of_distinct_foliar_leaf_spots

## Differential Diagnosis

### Similar Problems

- Bacterial Wilt / Moko Disease (*Ralstonia solanacearum*)
- Abiotic drought stress / Nutrient deficiency (Potassium)
- Banana Weevil Borer damage (*Cosmopolites abordi*)

### Diagnostic Discriminators

- Cut pseudostem observation: Fusarium wilt shows reddish-brown vascular discoloration WITHOUT bacterial fluid/ooze.
- Pattern of wilt: Fusarium starts on older leaves first; Moko disease often causes rapid wilting in young shoots and suckers first.
- Fruit internal symptoms: Fusarium does NOT cause internal brown rot of fruit pulp; Moko/Bacterial wilt frequently rots the fruit internally.

## Environmental Conditions

### Temperature

- 25°C to 30°C (Favors fungal proliferation and expression of vascular collapse)

### Humidity

- Not directly dependent; expressed severely during wet-to-dry weather transitions

### Rainfall / Moisture

- Soil moisture favors spore survival and root infection; waterlogging increases plant stress and infection susceptibility

### Soil

- Acidic soils, sandy loam soils, and soils with poor microbial diversity favor Fusarium persistence (survives 30+ years as chlamydospores)

## Crop Stage

- Expressed predominantly from vegetative growth through flowering and bunch maturation.
