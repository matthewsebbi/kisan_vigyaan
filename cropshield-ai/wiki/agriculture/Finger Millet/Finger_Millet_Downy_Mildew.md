# Finger Millet Downy Mildew

## Diagnostic Phenotype

### Primary Affected Plant Parts

- leaf_blade
- whole_plant
- earhead
- panicle

### Symptom Class

- systemic_disease
- diffuse_chlorosis
- green_ear
- floral_malformation

### Lesion / Symptom Geometry

- Primary shape: `none_no_distinct_lesion`
- Elongation: `high_at_leaf_level`
- Width profile: `continuous_or_diffuse`
- Ends: `not_applicable`
- Discrete or continuous: `continuous_diffuse_on_leaf`

### Size

- Pale-yellow translucent symptoms may occur as spots or areas on multiple leaves.
- As systemic infection progresses, affected plants may show extensive chlorosis and abnormal vegetative growth.
- During grain formation, the entire earhead may be transformed into narrow green leafy structures.

### Color

- Center / primary color: `pale_yellow_to_yellow_green`
- Margin: `not_applicable_or_gradual`
- Surrounding tissue: green
- Color transition: `gradual`
- Current/overall colors: pale_yellow, yellow, yellow_green, green

### Texture

- Current texture: smooth_on_chlorotic_leaves; leafy_and_soft_on_green_ear
- Early appearance: `pale_yellow_translucent_spots`

### Pattern and Distribution

- Orientation: `longitudinal_or_diffuse_on_leaf`
- Distribution: `continuous_or_diffuse`
- Coalescence: `extensive_with_systemic_progression`
- Vein relationship: `not_applicable_or_follows_leaf_axis`

### Spatial Location

- Primary location: `leaf_blade_and_whole_plant`
- Later diagnostic location: `earhead_panicle`

## Hallmark Features

- systemic_infection
- stunted_plants_with_shortened_internodes
- profuse_tillering
- bunchy_bushy_growth
- pale_yellow_translucent_leaf_spots_or_chlorosis
- green_ear_at_grain_formation
- earhead_completely_or_partially_transformed_into_narrow_green_leafy_structures
- complete_sterility
- characteristic_green_ear_appearance

## Negative / Exclusion Features

These features should reduce confidence in this diagnosis when they are clearly present.

- spindle_diamond_necrotic_leaf_lesions_as_primary_symptom
- numerous_small_brown_leaf_spots_as_primary_symptom
- raised_powdery_rust_pustules
- honeydew_exudation_from_florets
- dark_smut_spore_balls
- normal_undistorted_earhead_with_only_local_leaf_spots

## Differential Diagnosis

### Similar Problems

- Finger Millet Blast
- Finger Millet Brown Spot
- Finger Millet Leaf Spot
- Finger Millet Rust
- Other causes of earhead malformation

### Diagnostic Discriminators

- Identify the affected plant part before interpreting leaf color.
- Give high diagnostic weight to systemic stunting, shortened internodes, profuse tillering, and the bunchy appearance of infected plants.
- Distinguish pale-yellow translucent systemic symptoms from discrete necrotic lesions of blast or brown spot.
- At grain formation, inspect the earhead for transformation into narrow green leafy structures.
- Distinguish the green-ear transformation from discrete smut structures or brown/black blast damage on fingers.
- Treat white or pale sporulation on leaves as supporting evidence when visible, not as the sole diagnostic feature.
- Treat uncertainty in earhead transformation or plant-part identification as uncertainty rather than as a fact.

## Environmental Conditions

### Temperature

- Disease development is favored by cool conditions; the IIMR finger millet guide reports about 20–25°C during night and early morning as favorable for spore germination and disease development.

### Humidity

- Heavy dew favors disease development.

### Rainfall / Moisture

- Moisture and persistent dew during crop development can favor infection.

### Soil

- Good drainage is recommended in lowland areas; exact soil thresholds were not specified as diagnostic values.

## Crop Stage

- Can be expressed during plant development following systemic infection.
- The most diagnostically distinctive stage is grain formation, when the earhead develops the characteristic green-ear phenotype.
