# Cashew Anthracnose

## Diagnostic Phenotype

### Primary Affected Plant Parts

- young_flush_leaves
- tender_shoots
- inflorescence_panicles
- cashew_apples
- nuts

### Symptom Class

- necrotic_blight_and_dieback
- sunken_canker
- flower_and_fruit_rot
- resinous_exudation_on_twigs

### Lesion / Symptom Geometry

- Primary shape: `irregular_to_angular_depressed_lesions`
- Elongation: `moderate_along_panicle_axes_and_twigs`
- Width profile: `variable`
- Ends: `tapered_or_coalescing`
- Discrete or continuous: `discrete_initially_rapidly_becoming_continuous`

### Size

- Leaf spots: 2–10 mm. Panicle and fruit lesions: 5 mm to complete coverage of whole panicle/apple (over 100 mm).

### Color

- Center / primary color: dark_reddish_brown_to_black
- Margin: dark_purplish_brown
- Surrounding tissue: pale_yellowish_chlorotic
- Color transition: `abrupt`
- Current/overall colors: dark_brown, jet_black, salmon_pink_spore_masses, tan

### Texture

- Current texture: dry_hard_cankers_on_nuts, brittle_shriveled_flowers, slimy_pinkish_spore_pustules_in_high_humidity
- Early appearance: `water_soaked_reddish_brown_pinpoint_lesion`

### Pattern and Distribution

- Orientation: `along_panicle_branches_and_leaf_veins`
- Distribution: `widespread_on_meristematic_and_reproductive_tissues`
- Coalescence: `extensive_leading_to_blossom_blight`
- Vein relationship: `starts_along_main_and_secondary_leaf_veins`

### Spatial Location

- Primary location: `growing_tips_flushes_flowering_panicles_and_developing_fruits`

### Whole-Plant / Field-Level Features

- panicle_drying_and_blossom_blight
- twig_dieback_and_shepherd_crook_bending
- scorched_blackened_inflorescence
- mummified_young_nuts_and_apples
- heavy_yield_loss

## Hallmark Features

- rapid_blackening_and_blight_of_flowering_panicles
- reddish_brown_water_soaked_lesions_on_tender_flushes_and_nuts
- presence_of_salmon_pink_orange_conidial_masses_under_humid_conditions
- twig_dieback_with_small_resinous_gummy_droplets_near_cankers

## Negative / Exclusion Features

These features should reduce confidence in this diagnosis when they are clearly present:

- symptoms_restricted_strictly_to_thick_bark_of_main_trunk_base
- powdery_white_dusting_over_leaves_and_flowers
- yellow_orange_longitudinal_leaf_streaks_without_necrosis
- large_galls_or_rust_pustules_on_underside_of_leaf

## Differential Diagnosis

### Similar Problems

- Tea Mosquito Bug (*Helopeltis* spp.) damage
- Powdery Mildew (*Oidium anacardii*)
- Gummosis (*Lasiodiplodia theobromae*)

### Diagnostic Discriminators

- *Helopeltis* feeding damage causes distinct, elongated, dark feeding scabs centered on feeding punctures often with a clear yellow halo, whereas Anthracnose (*Colletotrichum gloeosporioides*) causes spreading rot with pinkish spore oozing.
- Powdery mildew produces white powdery fungal mats, unlike the dark necrotic rots of Anthracnose.
- Stem gummosis affects main trunks/scaffold limbs, while Anthracnose dieback is limited to apical twigs, shoots, and inflorescences.

## Environmental Conditions

### Temperature

- 25°C – 30°C optimal for *Colletotrichum* infection.

### Humidity

- High relative humidity (> 85%–95%) required for abundant spore production and infection.

### Rainfall / Moisture

- Intermittent rain showers during flush and flowering periods trigger severe blossom blight epidemics.

### Soil

- Applies across all soil types; over-fertilization with nitrogen increases succulent tissue vulnerability.

## Crop Stage

- Flowering and fruiting stage, early flush/vegetative emergence.
