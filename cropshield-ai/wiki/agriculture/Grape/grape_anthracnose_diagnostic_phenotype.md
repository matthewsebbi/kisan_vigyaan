# Grape Anthracnose

## Diagnostic Phenotype

### Primary Affected Plant Parts

- leaf_blade
- leaf_petiole
- young_shoots
- tendrils
- berries

### Symptom Class

- sunken_crater_like_cankers
- shot_hole_foliar_perforation
- bird_eye_fruit_spots
- shoot_dieback_and_stunting

### Lesion / Symptom Geometry

- Primary shape: `circular_to_elliptical_sunken_canker`
- Elongation: `moderate_along_stems_and_leaf_veins`
- Width profile: `narrow_to_expanding_with_raised_rim`
- Ends: `tapered_or_distinctly_bordered`
- Discrete or continuous: `discrete_spots_frequently_causing_shot_holes`

### Size

- Leaf spots: 1 mm to 5 mm in diameter. Fruit "bird's-eye" spots: 3 mm to 8 mm. Stem cankered fissures: up to 10–20 mm.

### Color

- Center / primary color: pale_grey_to_ash_white
- Margin: dark_reddish_brown_to_violet_black
- Surrounding tissue: yellowish_chlorotic_halo
- Color transition: `sharp_and_abrupt`
- Current/overall colors: ash_grey, dark_purple, reddish_brown, burnt_black

### Texture

- Current texture: dry, brittle, papery_center_dropping_out, sunken_hard_canker_rim
- Early appearance: `small_reddish_brown_circular_spots_on_young_leaves_and_shoots`

### Pattern and Distribution

- Orientation: `elongated_along_shoot_axes_and_leaf_veins`
- Distribution: `scattered_discrete_sunken_lesions`
- Coalescence: `moderate_on_young_stems`
- Vein relationship: `lesions_on_leaves_frequently_follow_and_distort_primary_veins`

### Spatial Location

- Primary location: `leaf_blades_petioles_succulent_shoots_and_green_berries`

### Whole-Plant / Field-Level Features

- shot_hole_leaf_appearance
- bird_eye_pattern_on_developing_berries
- deep_cankers_causing_shoot_snapping_and_dieback
- stunted_distorted_foliage
- severe_yield_reduction

## Hallmark Features

- distinctive_birds_eye_spots_on_berries_grey_center_with_dark_reddish_purple_border
- papery_centers_of_leaf_spots_drying_and_dropping_out_leaving_shot_hole_perforations
- sunken_deep_cankers_on_young_shoots_giving_a_burnt_or_hailed_upon_appearance
- distorted_curled_leaves_due_to_unilateral_vein_necrosis

## Negative / Exclusion Features

These features should reduce confidence in this diagnosis when they are clearly present:

- superficial_white_or_grey_powdery_mats_easily_wiped_off
- oily_yellow_translucent_spots_with_white_downy_growth_underneath
- large_diffuse_yellowing_of_entire_canopy_without_necrosis
- soft_water_soaked_grey_mold_rot_covering_harvest_ripe_berries

## Differential Diagnosis

### Similar Problems

- Hail Damage
- Black Rot (*Guignardia bidwellii*)
- Phomopsis Cane and Leaf Spot (*Phomopsis viticola*)
- Grape Powdery / Downy Mildew

### Diagnostic Discriminators

- Anthracnose lesions on shoots are **deeply sunken with raised dark purple borders**, whereas Phomopsis causes shallow, dark, elongated lesions that do not form deep craters.
- Leaf lesions drop out to create a distinct **shot-hole** effect, which does not occur in Downy or Powdery Mildew.
- Hail causes physical splitting on one side of canes without the characteristic dark reddish-purple raised margins seen in Anthracnose cankered lesions.

## Environmental Conditions

### Temperature

- 20°C – 30°C optimal for fungal infection.

### Humidity

- High humidity (> 85%) accelerates conidial production.

### Rainfall / Moisture

- Heavy spring rainfall and extended leaf wetness (minimum 12 hours) are essential for spore discharge from overwintering sclerotia/cankers.

### Soil

- Poorly drained, low-lying areas in vineyards with prolonged dew retention exacerbate disease severity.

## Crop Stage

- Early spring flush, tender shoot elongation, pre-bloom, and early berry development.
