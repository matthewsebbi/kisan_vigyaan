# Sunflower Head Rot

## Diagnostic Phenotype

### Primary Affected Plant Parts

- flower_head_capitulum
- receptacle
- seed_bracts
- developing_seeds

### Symptom Class

- wet_head_rot
- receptacle_soft_rot
- fungal_mycelial_growth
- seed_decay_and_shattering

### Lesion / Symptom Geometry

- Primary shape: `circular_expanding_to_irregular_mass`
- Elongation: `radial_and_transverse`
- Width profile: `expanding_to_cover_entire_head`
- Ends: `irregular_diffuse`
- Discrete or continuous: `continuous_expanding_rot`

### Size

- Can start as localized 2–5 cm soft spots on the back of the head (receptacle) and expand to cover the entire flowering head (15–30 cm in diameter).

### Color

- Center / primary color: brown, water_soaked_tan, grayish_brown, bleached_white
- Margin: dark_brown_to_olive
- Surrounding tissue: pale_yellow_chlorotic
- Color transition: `gradual_to_moderate`
- Current/overall colors: tan, brown, black_sclerotia, white_or_grayish_fungal_mass

### Texture

- Current texture: soft, pulpy, water_soaked, fibrous, shredded, stringy
- Early appearance: `water_soaked_soft_brown_spot_on_back_of_head`

### Pattern and Distribution

- Orientation: `radial_spreading_from_infection_point`
- Distribution: `localized_expanding_to_entire_head`
- Coalescence: `extensive`
- Vein relationship: `not_applicable`

### Spatial Location

- Primary location: `back_of_head_receptacle_and_front_seed_face`

### Whole-Plant / Field-Level Features

- head_drooping_and_detachment
- seed_shattering_and_loss
- white_cottony_mycelium_or_gray_sporangia_mats
- black_sclerotia_embedded_in_seed_head_pith
- foul_or_musty_odor_from_rotting_tissue

## Hallmark Features

- soft_water_soaked_brown_decay_of_the_receptacle
- presence_of_dense_white_cottony_growth_or_grayish_whiskery_mold
- skeletonized_shredded_head_tissue_with_seeds_falling_out
- hard_black_sclerotia_forming_within_the_rotted_head_matrix

## Negative / Exclusion Features

These features should reduce confidence in this diagnosis when they are clearly present.

- isolated_raised_rust_pustules_on_foliage
- dark_concentric_target_rings_restricted_to_lower_leaf_blades
- healthy_heads_with_isolated_basal_soil_line_stem_rot

## Differential Diagnosis

### Similar Problems

- Sclerotinia Head Rot (*Sclerotinia sclerotiorum*)
- Rhizopus Head Rot (*Rhizopus head rot complex*)
- Botrytis Head Rot (*Botrytis cinerea*)
- Insect / Bird Damage (e.g., *Head Moth* damage facilitating secondary rots)

### Diagnostic Discriminators

- Sclerotinia Head Rot produces dense, white cottony mycelium and large, rigid black sclerotia, whereas Rhizopus produces a coarse, gray-black "whiskery" fungal mass with black pinhead-like sporangia.
- Botrytis forms a velvety gray mold layer without large rat-dropping-like sclerotia.

## Environmental Conditions

### Temperature

- Sclerotinia Head Rot prefers cool-to-moderate temperatures (18°C–24°C); Rhizopus favors warmer temperatures (25°C–35°C).

### Humidity

- High relative humidity (> 85–90%) during and after flowering.

### Rainfall / Moisture

- Persistent rainfall, prolonged fog, or overhead irrigation during flowering (R-5) through seed development (R-7/R-8) retains water in the capitulum base.

### Soil

- High soil nitrogen content causing dense canopies and delayed maturity increases susceptibility.

## Crop Stage

- Flowering (R-5) to full maturity/harvest (R-9).
