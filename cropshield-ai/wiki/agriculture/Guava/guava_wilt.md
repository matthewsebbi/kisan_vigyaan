## Differential Diagnosis

### Similar Problems

- Guava Anthracnose
- Phytophthora Root Rot
- Nematode Damage (Meloidogyne spp.)

### Diagnostic Discriminators

- Vascular browning when roots or lower stems are cut open is indicative of wilt rather than anthracnose or surface foliar diseases.
- Rapid wilting and defoliation without distinct necrotic spots on fruits or leaves differentiates Fusarium/Macrophomina wilt from Anthracnose.
- Check root system integrity and vascular tissue color to distinguish systemic wilt from localized foliar blights.

## Environmental Conditions

### Temperature

- Warm temperatures (25°C to 35°C) favor disease development and rapid wilt onset.

### Humidity

- High atmospheric humidity coupled with soil moisture fluctuations accelerates wilt expression.

### Rainfall / Moisture

- Waterlogging or heavy rainfall in poorly drained soils enhances pathogen proliferation and root penetration.

### Soil

- Alkaline soils, heavy clay soils, or soils infested with root-knot nematodes increase severity.

## Crop Stage

- Can affect trees at any age, but most destructive on mature, fruit-bearing guava trees (3 to 15 years old).
