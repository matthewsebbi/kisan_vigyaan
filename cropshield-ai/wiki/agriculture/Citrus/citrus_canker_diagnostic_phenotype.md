# Citrus Canker (*Xanthomonas citri* subsp. *citri*)

## Diagnostic Phenotype

### Primary Affected Plant Parts

- leaf_blade
- stem_twigs
- fruit

### Symptom Class

- raised_corky_lesion

### Lesion / Symptom Geometry

- Primary shape: `circular_to_irregular`
- Elongation: `low_to_moderate`
- Width profile: `discrete`
- Ends: `rounded`
- Discrete or continuous: `discrete`

### Size

- 2 mm to 10 mm in diameter; lesions can coalesce into larger scabby patches on fruit and stems.

### Color

- Center / primary color: tan_to_brown_corky
- Margin: oily_water_soaked_border
- Surrounding tissue: bright_yellow_halo
- Color transition: `abrupt`
- Current/overall colors: brown, tan, yellow, water_soaked_green

### Texture

- Current texture: rough, corky, crater_like, raised, erumpent
- Early appearance: `pinpoint_water_soaked_spots`

### Pattern and Distribution

- Orientation: `random`
- Distribution: `scattered_or_coalescing`
- Coalescence: `moderate`
- Vein relationship: `independent`

### Spatial Location

- Primary location: `both_foliar_surfaces_stems_and_fruit`

### Whole-Plant / Field-Level Features

- premature_leaf_drop
- twig_dieback
- premature_fruit_drop
- blemished_unmarketable_fruit

## Hallmark Features

- raised_corky_erumpent_lesions
- oily_water_soaked_margins
- prominent_yellow_halo_on_leaves
- crater_like_sunken_centers_in_older_lesions

## Negative / Exclusion Features

These features should reduce confidence in this diagnosis when they are clearly present:

- soft_watery_bark_rot
- powdery_white_mycelial_growth
- asymmetrical_blotchy_mottle
- gum_exudation_from_trunk

## Differential Diagnosis

### Similar Problems

- Citrus Scab (*Elsinoë fawcettii*)
- Melanose (*Diaporthe citri*)
- Citrus Greening (*Huanglongbing*)

### Diagnostic Discriminators

- Citrus Scab lacks the prominent oily water-soaked margin and distinct yellow halo characteristic of Citrus Canker.
- Melanose produces small, dark brown, sand-paper-like raised specks without sunken crater-like centers.

## Environmental Conditions

### Temperature

- 20°C – 30°C optimal for disease development.

### Humidity

- High relative humidity (> 80%) accelerates bacterial multiplication and infection.

### Rainfall / Moisture

- Free moisture and wind-driven rain are critical for bacterial dispersal and stomatal ingress.

### Soil

- Not directly soil-borne; transmission relies on wind, rain splash, and mechanical contact.

## Crop Stage

- All stages with actively growing young vegetative flushes and developing fruits are highly susceptible.
